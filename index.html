The Product Function

The function of Product is to maximize the value-exchange cycle that an organization develops between itself and its customers.

Product Management promotes valuable outcomes and companies that excel in product management are over 40% more likely to beat the Rule of 40 performance metric.

Because of difficult access to capital, Software players are compelled to adjust their efforts. Gone are the days of unlimited spending and expansive development teams producing features without restraint. Today, the technology industry confronts a rigorous economic landscape, making each investment in development and every feature launch a calculated move to ensure growth and profitability. Simultaneously, the software industry is infamous for its high project failure rates. According to the Standish Group’s Chaos Report, only 29% of software projects succeed, while 52% face challenges, and 19% are considered complete failures. Reasons range from misalignment between R&D and business goals to ineffective pricing strategies. Now more than ever, software companies need a disciplined approach to product development—one that prioritizes a deep understanding of the market, meticulous feature selection, and strategic alignment with fundamental business objectives.

People and Process Problems

Over 70% of companies struggle with lack of alignment between market expectations and product strategy throughout the product lifecycle, from market introduction to end-of-life.

By and large, this is attempted through people; People with varying degrees of training and professional rigour, fallibly and inconsistently applying frameworks and processes, achieving wildly unreliable outcomes.

Despite significant spending as a proportion of ARR (20-30% on avg), only 25% of chief technology officers say the return on their company’s software development investments meets expectations.

"Doing product” is a means to an end, not an end in and of itself. Enter Product Management as a System: Systematizing the practice of product management.

Objectives

Software Product Management executives are reporting high and worsening PM attrition. If your entire organization operates within a system of Product Management, you no longer rely on individuals to sustain best-practices.

  • Customer Centricity
  • Strategic Roadmaps
  • Empowerment
  • Engineering Collaboration
  • Marketing Collaboration
  • Product Life-Cycle
  • Shared Best-Practices

Product management is no longer primarily the purview of the head of product; at leading companies, it’s top of mind for the entire C-suite and board of directors. They realize it can play a powerful role in directing engineering effort, maximizing precious R&D resources, eliminating low-value investments, and, ultimately, achieving and sustaining financial growth that outperforms the competition. Add the rest of your organization to the strategic human-capital stack to create the most resilient Product Management system.
